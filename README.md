Cybersecurity risk assessment and security improvement plan for a simulated small business environment.
Project Overview
This project is a simulated cybersecurity risk assessment for a small business environment.
The purpose of the assessment is to identify common cybersecurity risks affecting a small organization and recommend practical security controls that can reduce the likelihood and impact of security incidents.
The assessment focuses on:
Password security and multi-factor authentication
User access controls
Backup strategy
Phishing risks
Security improvement recommendations
Simulated Business Environment
Business: Evergreen Wellness Studio
Employees: 8
Technology Environment:
Windows computers
Microsoft 365
Cloud-based scheduling software
Business email
Cloud file storage
Wi-Fi network
Online payment processing
Social media accounts
This is a simulated environment created for educational and portfolio purposes. No real customer, employee, or business data was used.
1. Password Security & Multi Factor Authentication
Risk
Weak, reused, or compromised passwords can allow unauthorized access to business systems.
A compromised password could potentially provide access to email, cloud applications, financial systems, customer information, or social media accounts.
Recommended Controls
Require unique passwords for each account.
Enable multi factor authentication on critical accounts.
Prioritize MFA for email, financial, administrative, and social media accounts.
Use a reputable password manager.
Avoid shared employee accounts.
Establish a process for changing credentials when an employee leaves the organization.
Priority
High
2. Access Controls
Risk
Employees may have access to systems or information that they do not need to perform their jobs.
Excessive permissions increase the potential impact of a compromised account.
Recommended Controls
Give employees individual accounts.
Apply the principle of least privilege.
Review user permissions regularly.
Remove access when an employee leaves.
Use separate administrator accounts for administrative tasks.
Maintain an inventory of important business systems and authorized users.
Priority
High
3. Backup Strategy
Risk
Important business information could be lost because of accidental deletion, hardware failure, ransomware, or account compromise.
Backups are only useful if they can actually be restored.
Recommended Controls
Maintain regular backups of important business information.
Protect backups from unauthorized access.
Maintain more than one copy of important information.
Periodically test restoration procedures.
Document who is responsible for backups and recovery.
Priority
High
4. Phishing Risks
Risk
Employees may receive fraudulent emails or messages designed to steal passwords, financial information, or other sensitive data.
Recommended Controls
Provide regular phishing awareness training.
Teach employees how to identify suspicious links, attachments, and requests.
Establish a simple process for reporting suspicious messages.
Require verification of unusual financial or credential requests.
Enable available email security protections.
Priority
Medium
5. Recommended Security Improvements
Priority	Recommendation
High,	enable MFA on critical accounts
High,	eliminate shared passwords and accounts
High,	review administrator privileges
High,	establish and test backups
Medium,	conduct regular access reviews
Medium,	implement phishing-awareness training
Medium,	establish an employee off-boarding process
Implementation Plan
First 7 Days
Enable MFA on critical accounts.
Review administrator accounts.
Identify inactive accounts.
Change weak or compromised passwords.
Confirm that important business data is being backed up.
Within 30 Days
Review employee permissions.
Establish a formal backup procedure.
Create a phishing-reporting process.
Provide basic security-awareness training.
Document important business systems and responsible users.
Within 90 Days
Test backup restoration.
Conduct another access review.
Review MFA coverage.
Update security procedures based on lessons learned.
Create a basic incident response procedure.
Key Takeaways
The assessment demonstrates how a small business can reduce common cybersecurity risks through practical controls such as multi-factor authentication, least-privilege access, reliable backups, employee security awareness, and regular access reviews.
The most important lesson is that effective cybersecurity is not only about technology. It also depends on people, processes, documentation, and consistent operational practices.
